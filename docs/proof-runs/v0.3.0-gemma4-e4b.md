# Proof run: v0.3.0 on gemma4:e4b

What happened when Saddlefit fitted a harness for a real compact model on a generated task suite. The goal for v0.3.0 was to show that the whole loop works end to end without defects: task generation, baseline, fit with its gates, and the closing report. A large gain was not the goal. This document records the numbers, the defects the run found, and what they mean for the next versions.

## Setup

- **Student:** `gemma4:e4b` in Ollama on a laptop. Native tool calls, 32k context, temperature 0.2, seed 7.
- **Tutor and generator:** `claude-opus-5-5`.
- **Repository:** Saddlefit's own code.
- **Suite:** 74 generated tasks, 54 train and 20 held-out (50 bugfix, 24 feature). Splits come from the HMAC of each task id. Each task was kept only if the student solved it in 1 to 4 of 5 calibration runs. Every overlap between splits was removed by the repeat guard and an operator audit.
- **Decisions:** 3 runs per task, solved at 2 of 3. The held-out gate is a paired sign-flip test on pass counts, with α = 0.05 divided by the round's candidates (B12).

## Results

All measurements below are on the same 74 tasks with 3 runs per task.

| Measurement | Harness | Train solved | Held-out solved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline, before #174 (student blind, see below) | baseline | 24/54 (44%) | 17/20 (85%) |
| Baseline, after #174 | baseline | 30/54 (56%) | 17/20 (85%) |
| Held-out re-measure | baseline | — | 15/20 (75%) |
| Closing evaluation, fit with lessons in the student's context | baseline + 6 lessons | 32/54 (59%) | 12/20 (60%) |
| **Closing evaluation, final fit** | **`max_chars` 12000 → 20000** | **35/54 (65%)** | **14/20 (70%)** |

For the final fit against the baseline, the paired bootstrap 95% intervals are −4% to +22% on train and −35% to +5% on held-out. Neither excludes zero.

**The accepted change.** The final fit tried nine candidates in three rounds. It accepted one: `tool_output.max_chars` from 12000 to 20000, so that the student reads whole source files instead of truncated ones. On the round's training batch it raised solved tasks from 2 to 4 of 6. On held-out it turned 2 tasks from unsolved to solved and none the other way, and its pass-count gain passed the paired test at α = 0.05/3.

**Reading the numbers.** The same baseline harness scored 17/20 and 15/20 on held-out in two measurements. A swing of two tasks is ordinary noise for this model at 3 runs per task. Against that range, the final harness's 14/20 is no measurable change on held-out. Train rose by 5 tasks, but the interval includes zero. The gate accepted a change that won its own paired comparison. The closing evaluation against the baseline cannot confirm a gain, and it does not show a loss either.

Tutor cost over all fits: 76 calls, $4.05. The final fit cost $0.95 for 18 calls. Generation cost roughly $20 of Opus 5.5 calls. The exact total was not recorded, because the last generation command was stopped before its summary.

## Defects the run found, all fixed in v0.3.0

| PR | Defect | How it showed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| #168 | Held-out decided by wins − losses on one run | The first fit "accepted" `max_chars` 4000 → 12000. Re-measured at 5 runs: 73% before, 70% after. |
| #161, #170, #171, #173 | Generated tasks repeated each other across splits; the generator lost sight of old tasks | 4 of the first 6 held-out tasks repeated train tasks. The generator kept rewriting the same bugs. |
| #166 | The report's "before" came from a fit run, not the baseline | The report and the baseline disagreed. |
| #174 | The student's sandbox had no `PYTHONPATH`, unlike the evaluator | 169 of 211 train runs saw `ModuleNotFoundError` instead of their test results. The tutor spent three rounds working around it. |
| #177 | The tutor's lessons reached the student's context without a gate | Lessons were the only change in one fit. Held-out went from 15–17/20 to 12/20. |
| #178 | The tutor wrote diffs by hand; ruff's EXE001 refused tool scripts; the `tools/` rules were never stated | Two of one fit's three rounds tested no candidate. |
| #187 | An empty run record stopped `report` | A fit stopped right after creating a run record left an empty file. |

Each fix has a test that fails on the old behaviour.

## What the run shows

- **The gates hold.** Three times a candidate won its training batch clearly (4 → 5, 4 → 5, 4 → 6 of 6) and lost or tied on held-out. One of them, a `tools/syntax_check.py` hook, scored 1 win against 5 losses. A gate on train alone would have accepted all three.
- **The tutor learns from refusals.** It stopped retrying a refused workaround and moved from text rules to loop mechanics and tool scripts. Between rounds, it narrowed one idea into variants.
- **The limits are measurement, not mechanics.** At 20 held-out tasks and 3 runs, only large gains can pass, and the baseline's own noise is about two tasks. A training batch of 6 tasks lets candidates fit those six tasks.

## Next

- **v0.4.0:** sequential tests, so clear cases stop early and runs go where they decide something. Recalibrating a suite for a new student (#172). The slow loop.
- **v0.5.0:** parallel runs through more runners. At about 3 minutes a run, a fit plus its closing evaluation took two to three days on one laptop.
- **Measurement:** more held-out tasks or more runs per task before claiming small gains. Larger training batches, so the tutor's changes cannot fit a handful of tasks.
